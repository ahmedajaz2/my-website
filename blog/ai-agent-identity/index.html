🤖 Identity Security for AI Agents

🗓️ Published: October 2026 | ✍️ Author: Ajaz Ahmed

Somewhere in your environment right now, an AI agent is reading emails, querying databases, or pushing code — and nobody provisioned its identity. Agents are the fastest-growing population of identities in the enterprise, and most identity teams cannot answer four basic questions about them: where are my agents, what can they do, what are they doing, and how do I respond when one goes rogue? Here is the identity-first playbook I would run.

🤖 Agents Are Identities — Treat Them Like It

Every agent is a non-human identity with credentials, permissions, and a lifecycle. The mistake I keep seeing: teams treat agents like features, not identities. If it can authenticate and take action, it belongs in your identity governance — same discipline as a service account, only more dynamic and much harder to see.

📋 Question 1: Where Are My Agents?

You cannot govern what you cannot see. Agents spawn from Copilot Studio, Azure AI Foundry, LangChain apps, RPA bots with an LLM bolted on, and shadow-IT experiments nobody told you about. Build the inventory first: require registration before an agent gets credentials. Microsoft’s agent registry in Entra exists for exactly this reason. No registration, no identity, no access.

🔑 Question 2: What Can They Authenticate As?

Give every agent its own identity — never shared credentials. Managed identities in Azure, workload identity federation everywhere else, short-lived tokens instead of long-lived secrets sitting in config files. If an agent’s credential looks like something a person could copy and paste, you have already lost.

🎯 Question 3: What Can They Do?

Least privilege is harder for agents because their “job” is fuzzy. Scope tokens to the exact APIs and data they need, prefer just-in-time elevation over standing access, and put a human approval step in front of destructive or exfiltration-capable actions. An agent that needs to read the CRM does not need to export it.

👀 Question 4: What Are They Doing?

Log every agent action the way you would log a privileged user: what it accessed, what it changed, what it sent out. Baseline normal behavior so anomalies stand out — an agent suddenly reading ten times its usual records at 2am is your canary. Feed agent logs into your SIEM with the agent’s identity attached, not just the application name.

🔄 Govern the Whole Lifecycle

Agents need joiner-mover-leaver too. Assign every agent a human sponsor who is accountable for it. Review agent permissions on a schedule — quarterly at minimum. And deprovision aggressively: an agent nobody has used in 90 days is attack surface, not an asset. Disable first, delete after a grace period.

💬 Closing Thoughts

The agent identity problem is the service-account problem with a growth rate. The teams that win will be the ones that apply boring identity fundamentals — inventory, unique identities, least privilege, lifecycle — to the new population instead of inventing a parallel universe. Start with the inventory; everything else follows.

  • ✅ Inventory every agent and require registration before issuing credentials.
  • ✅ One identity per agent — managed identity or federation, no shared secrets.
  • ✅ Short-lived, tightly scoped tokens; just-in-time elevation for anything sensitive.
  • ✅ Human approval gates on destructive or data-export actions.
  • ✅ Log agent actions with identity attached and feed them to your SIEM.
  • ✅ A human sponsor for every agent, with quarterly access reviews.
  • ✅ Auto-disable agents idle 90+ days; delete after a grace period.
